# Field-Validate — Turning a Scorecard into Field Evidence

> Mode D detail. The scorecard says what's unproven; this turns it into interviews and folds the results back into a re-score. PG-grounded and Mom-Test-native — no generic UX apparatus (no persona taxonomies, JTBD frameworks, prioritization matrices, or affinity diagrams; those would dilute the PG grounding). The founder runs the interviews; synthetic/AI participants are never a substitute for real ones in validation.

## 1. Map each open filter to a question

For every CONDITIONAL or FAIL filter, write the past-behavior questions whose answers would flip it. The load-bearing three:

- **ORGANIC** — "When did you first hit this problem yourself? Walk me through that day."
- **WELL** — "Tell me about the last time this happened to you. What did it cost — time, money, a scramble? What did you do instead?" (Listening for: behavior + money already flowing to a worse fix.)
- **BUCHHEIT** — "What are you using for this today? When did you last reach for it?"

The rest follow the same shape — a specific past event, never an opinion. SCHLEP → "what's the ugliest part of dealing with this?" SURVIVAL → "have you paid anyone to fix this? what's it on the budget as?"

## 2. The interview-craft pack

- **Story excavation.** When they generalize ("I usually…"), redirect: "Tell me about *one specific time*. Walk me through what happened." Specific incidents beat aspirations.
- **No leading questions.** Never "is this hard?" / "would you use…". Use "how would you describe…" / "walk me through what you did."
- **The 60-second rule.** After the opening prompt, wait. Silence pulls out the real answer; jumping in signals you want it short.
- **The "Oh?" technique.** The two-letter prompt "Oh?" surfaces more without biasing.
- **Master/apprentice frame.** "I'm here to learn from you — there are no right answers. We're testing the approach, not you." Get recording consent.
- **A "wrong" answer is a finding.** Never correct them — their mental model *is* the data.
- **Referral close.** "Who else deals with this who'd talk to me?" Snowball the next interviews.

## 3. The screener (behavior, not demographics)

2–4 questions, screen-outs first, no yes/no (people agree to qualify). Filter on what they *do*, not who they are — "which of these have you done in the last 30/90 days?" with specific options. End with one **articulacy check**: "Tell me about a recent time you [relevant activity]." A storied answer signals a good qualitative interview; a one-word answer signals a poor fit.

## 4. Synthesize the return into a re-score

When transcripts come back:

- Pull findings, each grounded in **≥2 independent real sources** — one vivid quote is a story, two is a pattern. A single source never flips a filter.
- Weight **behavior and money** over stated enthusiasm (the Mom-Test rule). "I'd totally use that" is noise; "I paid X / I rebuilt it in a spreadsheet / I escalated it twice" is signal.
- Re-score the affected filters under Mode C's anti-inflation rules. A CONDITIONAL moves to PASS only when the gap it named is now closed by that ≥2-source evidence — otherwise it stays CONDITIONAL with the gap restated.
- If a filter was FAIL and the field says the pain is real but elsewhere, that's a wedge correction — loop back to CREATE, don't paper over it.

## Output

`vision-fieldwork.md`: the research plan + screener + interview guide first; append the synthesized findings and the re-scored filters after the interviews are done.
